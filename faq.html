The problem: your website can't answer questions

Your site gets found. Then a visitor wants a price, a detail, a "do you do this?" — and there's nobody there to answer. They don't write it down for later. They leave.

What actually happens when a visitor can't get an answer?

They go back to the search results and pick the next business. A website visit is a moment of intent — someone actively looking for what you sell. When the question in that moment goes unanswered, the visit is gone, along with the job attached to it. A chatbot answers in that moment, while they're still on your site.

How much is one captured visitor worth?

Do your own math: your website visitors per month × the share who'd start a conversation if someone answered × your average job value × your close rate. For most local businesses, one extra booked customer a month covers the entire cost of Vellops — everything after that is work you were already losing.

Can't visitors just use my contact form or email me?

They can — almost none do. Forms ask visitors to do the work: compose a message, wait hours or days for a reply, hope it didn't land in spam. A chat conversation answers instantly, asks the follow-up questions for them, and captures their name, email and request while they're still interested.

What about after hours and weekends?

That's exactly when your chatbot earns its keep. People research plumbers, dentists, roofers and restaurants at night and on weekends. Your chatbot answers day and night — and the lead is waiting in your email inbox in the morning, already qualified, sometimes already booked.

What the chatbot actually does

It's not a pop-up with three canned answers. It's a trained member of your team, living on your website.

What kinds of questions can it answer?

Anything it's trained on from your business: services and products, pricing guidance, hours, service area, policies, booking steps, and your FAQs. It's built from your website and the details you give us — and it only answers from those approved facts. If a visitor asks something outside them, it captures their details and emails them to you instead of guessing.

Is it really customer service AND sales?

Yes — both, in the same conversation. Customer service: hours, policies, "what should I expect?", everyday support questions. Sales: qualifying what the visitor needs, explaining the right service, giving pricing guidance, handling the usual objections, and guiding them to book or buy. That's the job description it works from.

How does booking work?

On Standard and Premium, the chatbot books appointments straight into your calendar and emails the customer a confirmation. On Starter, it captures the lead — name, email, what they need, when they want — and emails it to you to confirm. Either way, no lead waits in a chat window: you get an email for every one.

How do I know what it's saying to my customers?

You approve the answers before anything goes live. Every conversation is saved, lead alerts and summaries land in your email, and your Monday report shows three numbers — chats handled, leads captured, booked — drawn from your real conversations.
